### 2.2 Design rationale and metrics — approved by Member 2 (8 November 2026; Confirm and Save rows updated 16 November 2026)

> **Review note:** This table was prepared as a proposed explanation, then reviewed and approved by Member 2 on 8 November 2026. Preparation assistance is disclosed in `AI-USAGE.md`. The funnel-conversion metrics below are not instrumented. The separate operational Owner Dashboard metrics are implemented on the Member 5 integration branch and documented in §4.

| Stage | Design rationale (why these elements suit this stage and customer) | Business metric influenced (not yet measured) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Browse** | Collectors and serious players compare instruments on authenticity before aesthetics. The verification badge and score therefore sit on every card, and the Verification filter is a first-class control (GC1 p. 4, feature 2). Peso price bands reflect the high-value catalog (GC1 p. 3). The "Model reference photo" label stops a sample image being mistaken for evidence, which fits a customer whose "primary friction is fear" (p. 1). Buyers of vintage instruments often search for a specific make or model. Autocomplete suggests only instruments that exist in the catalog and can open a detail page directly, shortening the route from Browse to Select. Verification details are left to the cards and detail page rather than repeated in suggestions. | Browse-to-detail click-through rate. Search-suggestion selection rate. Share of sessions using the Verification filter. Zero-result search rate. |
| **Select** | A high-value purchase needs inspection detail before commitment. The detail page therefore shows verification documentation, condition notes and seller identity on the same page as the price. Explicit copy that the cart does not reserve stock or take payment avoids false purchasing confidence. Stock-capped quantities prevent selections that cannot be fulfilled. | Detail-to-cart rate (add-to-cart actions ÷ detail views). Cart abandonment rate. |
| **Confirm** | A multi-step wizard follows the GC1 checkout design (p. 5) and keeps each step short. Inline, per-step validation is intended to reduce error-driven drop-off. Re-checking price and stock before review protects trust when listings are often one-of-one (seed stock = 1). Optional sign-in removes account creation as a barrier for a first purchase. Showing the verification fee and total from the server before "Place simulated order", and labelling payment as simulated, keeps the buyer's expectation of the saved order accurate. | Checkout step completion rate. Checkout abandonment by step. |
| **Save** | The confirmation screen is designed to show only server-saved data (reference, saved total, status), giving the buyer verifiable proof from `GET /api/orders/:id`. Restricting access to the ordering browser or account protects a buyer's purchase details. Showing escrow-stage labels without implying payment keeps the screen accurate until escrow exists (Final Project). | Order conversion rate (saved orders ÷ checkout starts). Feeds the owner metrics Total orders, Total revenue and Average order value (§4). |

### 3.3 Business conclusions

The 8% commission supports the marketplace model because ToneVault earns more when it helps complete higher-value guitar sales. The verification add-on supports the value proposition because buyers may pay for more confidence before purchasing. Keeping the add-on optional avoids raising every buyer's total.

GC2 stores a transparent snapshot instead of processing money. Therefore these amounts are **simulated or projected revenue**, not cash received. Actual earned-revenue reporting still needs the group's agreed escrow/status rule and Member 5's metrics work.

| Dashboard metric | Observed value (saved-data evidence) | Business decision | Rationale |

Tracking it over time helps the owner assess marketplace engagement. |
| Total order value | ₱1,930,800.00 | Monitor the gross value of saved orders to assess transaction activity and identify opportunities to increase buyer spending. | This represents the combined value of buyer orders. It is a measure of marketplace transaction volume, not income earned by ToneVault. |
| Platform revenue | ₱24,000.00 | Track the platform revenue generated by commissions and verification fees when evaluating the marketplace's income. | This metric uses the saved platform revenue amounts. It is distinct from total order value because the full value of a guitar transaction does not belong to ToneVault. |
| Average order value | ₱321,800.00 | Monitor changes in average spending per order to understand purchasing behavior and evaluate whether the marketplace is attracting buyers in its intended premium segment. | Average order value is calculated by dividing total order value by the number of saved orders. The observed value is ₱1,930,800 divided by 6 orders. |
| Top-selling instrument | Fender Telecaster — 2 units sold | Use the current sales result as an initial reference when deciding which guitar models to feature, source, or promote. Reassess as more transactions are recorded. | The Telecaster has the highest recorded quantity sold in the current dataset. This is an early signal, not enough evidence to establish overall market-wide demand. |
| Low-stock listings | 6 available listings at or below the threshold of 2 units | Review these listings and consider contacting sellers about replenishment or availability before promoting them further. | Low-stock monitoring helps the owner identify listings with limited remaining inventory. Since some pre-owned guitars may be one-of-a-kind, low stock does not automatically mean a seller should replenish the same model. |
### Metric interpretation and limitations

- **Total order value and platform revenue are different measures.** Total order value represents the gross value of saved buyer orders, while platform revenue represents the income recorded for ToneVault through commissions and verification fees.
- **Platform revenue depends on saved revenue records.** The dashboard sums the stored `platform_revenue_amount` values rather than estimating revenue from total order value. Historical orders with zero recorded platform revenue may therefore not reflect revenue that was never saved in the database.
- **Low-stock threshold:** The current implementation treats available listings with 2 or fewer units as low stock. This is a provisional threshold and should be confirmed with the group before final deployment.
- **Results are a snapshot.** These figures describe the saved data observed during testing and should not be interpreted as permanent totals or market-wide demand.
- **Test-data limitations:** The observed Fender Telecaster sales count of 2 units and the 6 low-stock listings reflect the saved dataset used during testing. They should not be treated as reliable evidence of market-wide demand.
